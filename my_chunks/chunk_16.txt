Section 23(2), in my view deals with the situation where a person does not refuse 
or fail to give consent to the procedure (thus avoiding any prosecution under s 23(1)) but 
does not co-operate in the physical processes of taking a blood sample – for instance, by 
refusing to keep still while the doctor or nurse finds a vein and inserts a needle. …
[32] Nor in my view did Mr Martyn commit an offence under s 23(2). Certainly he refused 
to consent to the taking of blood, but under s 17(2)(b), the effect of that refusal was to free 
the doctor from the obligation under s 15AA to take a blood sample; thus there was no 
continuing requirement under s 15AA for the sample to be taken, and no possible offence 
under s 23(2). 16. We respectfully disagree with these aspects of Penfold J’s opinion.
