Police officers, doctors or nurses are not given statutory 
power to physically compel drivers to give samples of blood, breath or saliva. Instead, 
such failure or refusal is itself made an offence. Thus, s 22 provides, relevantly, that a 
person who “has, in accordance with the provisions specified in this Act, been required 
to provide a sample of breath for breath analysis” commits an offence if “the person 
fails or refuses to provide a sample of breath” for analysis. There are corresponding 
offences where a person is required to, but fails or refuses to, provide an oral fluid 
sample for analysis (s 22A), or a person fails or refuses to undergo an alcohol 
screening test or drug screening test (ss 22B and 22C). 8.
